Recent finding · 2026

Butyrate activates sensory neurons through colonic serotonin release

Using a microfluidic co-culture model and real-time imaging, we identified a signaling pathway through which the microbial metabolite butyrate activates sensory neurons. Butyrate stimulates colonic epithelial cells via GPR41 and GPR43 to release serotonin, which engages 5-HT3a receptors on sensory neurons and elicits calcium influx, a marker of neuronal activation.

Research profile

I am a doctoral candidate in Biomedical Engineering at the University of Toledo. My research applies microphysiological systems to the study of cellular interactions, with a translational focus. I integrate microfluidic engineering, primary cell culture, and quantitative functional assays to examine how cell populations communicate under controlled, physiologically relevant conditions.

My work has resulted in four peer-reviewed publications, two as first author, and presentations at national scientific meetings. I am also committed to teaching, mentorship, and STEM outreach.

Research experience

Graduate Research Assistant, Department of Bioengineering

January 2021 to Present
University of Toledo, Biofluidics Lab, Toledo, Ohio
  • Engineered a tri-culture microfluidic model of the gut-brain axis that integrates gut microbes, epithelial cells, and sensory neurons, enabling real-time study of host-microbiome signaling. Operated and maintained more than 200 microfluidic devices.
  • Maintained four or more primary and established cell types, including colonic epithelial cells, sensory neurons, endothelial cells, and cancer cell lines, and adapted protocols across standard and microfluidic platforms.
  • Designed co-culture experiments that reproduce physiological tissue microenvironments and generate reproducible data for mechanistic studies.
  • Quantified epithelial barrier integrity and neuronal excitability using permeability assays, tight junction characterization, and real-time calcium imaging with single-cell analysis.
  • Characterized protein and gene expression across multi-cell-type models using immunofluorescence, Western blot, PCR and qPCR, and RNA extraction.
  • Applied quantitative image analysis at single-cell resolution to assess cell morphology, barrier integrity, and neuronal activity, contributing data to four peer-reviewed publications.
  • Developed and validated a transfection protocol for primary colonic epithelial cells within a microfluidic device, confirming expression by fluorescence imaging.
  • Performed cytokine profiling and ELISA to quantify inflammatory mediator secretion in co-culture systems under pharmacological stimulation.
  • Conducted reactive oxygen species, cell viability, and dose-response assays to characterize compound activity in microfluidic models.
  • Maintained long-term perfusion cultures under defined flow and dosing conditions, ensuring reproducibility across multi-week studies.
  • Developed a blood-brain barrier model incorporating brain microvascular endothelial cells and pericytes as a secondary research platform for the laboratory.
  • Mentored undergraduate and graduate students in device operation, cell culture, imaging, and experimental design.
