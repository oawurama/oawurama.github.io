About me

I am a PhD candidate in Biomedical Engineering at the University of Toledo, where I work in the Tang Lab building physiologically relevant in vitro models of the gut-brain axis. My research is driven by a central question: how does the microbial community living in our gut communicate with our nervous system, and what does that mean for health and disease?

To explore this, I developed a tri-culture microfluidic model that brings together intestinal epithelial cells, enteric neurons, and live gut microbiota on a chip, enabling real-time observation of host-microbiome crosstalk. This system is now the core experimental platform of an NIH/NHLBI R21-funded project on hypertension signaling.

Beyond the bench, I care deeply about teaching and mentorship. I have guided 100+ undergraduate students as a teaching assistant and received the Outstanding Teaching Assistant Award from the College of Engineering in 2025. I am graduating in December 2026 and am actively exploring opportunities in industry R&D, pharma, and academic research.

Research
Core focus

Gut-brain axis modeling

Developed a novel neuronal-epithelial-microbial tri-culture system to study how microbial metabolites activate enteric sensory neurons via epithelial serotonin release.

Platform

Organ-on-chip technology

Experienced operating and adapting commercial microfluidic platforms across 200+ devices for multi-cell-type culture, long-term pharmacological studies, and high-throughput functional assays.

Additional work

Cancer microenvironment

Contributed to biomimetic models of triple-negative breast cancer intravasation and metastasis, supporting drug efficacy screening in physiologically relevant tumor microenvironments.

Examining cells under the microscope in the Tang Lab, University of Toledo
