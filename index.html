About me

I am actively exploring opportunities across a wide range of fields -- from pharma and biotech to science communication, education, and beyond. If my background resonates with what you are building, I would love to connect.

I am a PhD candidate in Biomedical Engineering at the University of Toledo, where I work in the Tang Lab studying how the gut microbiome communicates with the nervous system. My research uses live cell imaging, tri-culture systems, and microfluidic platforms to observe host-microbiome interactions in real time.

My current project focuses on how microbial metabolites like butyrate activate enteric sensory neurons through serotonin signaling in intestinal epithelial cells. This work is supported by an NIH/NHLBI R21 grant and has been presented at national conferences including the American Physiological Society Annual Meeting.

I hold a BSc in Biomedical Engineering from Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology (Ghana, 2017).

Research
Core focus

Gut-brain axis signaling

Developed a tri-culture system combining intestinal epithelial cells, neurons, and gut microbiota to study how microbial metabolites activate sensory neurons via serotonin release.

Platform

Organ-on-chip technology

Experienced operating commercial microfluidic platforms across 200+ devices for multi-cell-type culture, pharmacological studies, and high-throughput functional assays.

Additional work

Cancer microenvironment

Contributed to biomimetic models of triple-negative breast cancer intravasation, supporting drug efficacy screening in physiologically relevant tumor microenvironments.

Teaching & mentorship

One of my favorite parts of this work is the people.

I genuinely love working with students. Whether I am explaining a concept for the fifth time, helping someone work through a tricky problem, or just being a steady presence in a stressful semester, I find deep joy in those moments of connection and growth.

As a teaching assistant at the University of Toledo, I have supported 100+ undergraduate students across multiple semesters in Biomedical Engineering courses. In 2025 I received the Outstanding Teaching Assistant Award from the College of Engineering -- an honor that means a lot to me because it came from the students I care about most.

I have also informally mentored undergraduates, a graduate student, and high school students in the lab, and I hope mentorship remains a big part of whatever comes next for me.
